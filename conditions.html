Areas of Focus

Comprehensive Pain Categories

We categorize conditions by their origin to ensure we target the exact mechanism causing your pain.

Spine & Musculoskeletal

Structural issues affecting the bones, joints, muscles, and connective tissues. These are the most common causes of chronic pain.

  • Herniated Discs & Sciatica
  • Spinal Stenosis & Facet Arthritis
  • Osteoarthritis & Joint Pain
  • Tendonitis & Myofascial Pain

Neuropathic Pain

Pain caused by damage or dysfunction of the nervous system itself. This often feels like burning, tingling, or electric shocks.

  • Complex Regional Pain Syndrome (CRPS)
  • Diabetic Peripheral Neuropathy
  • Post-Herpetic Neuralgia (Shingles)
  • Trigeminal Neuralgia

Headaches & Facial Pain

Debilitating head and facial pain syndromes that require specialized neurological and interventional management.

  • Chronic Migraines
  • Cluster Headaches
  • Temporomandibular Joint (TMJ) Dysfunction
  • Occipital Neuralgia

Post-Surgical & Trauma

Pain that persists after a surgical procedure or physical trauma, often requiring specialized scar tissue management.

  • Failed Back Surgery Syndrome
  • Post-Mastectomy Pain Syndrome
  • Phantom Limb Pain
  • Joint Replacement Pain
Most Common Condition

Understanding Lower
Back Pain

Lower back pain is the leading cause of disability worldwide. It can be caused by muscle strain, herniated discs, spinal stenosis, or facet joint arthritis. The pain can be localized or radiate down the legs (sciatica).

At PainRelief, we don't just prescribe rest and medication. We use advanced diagnostics to find the exact structural or neurological cause, and apply targeted treatments like epidural injections or radiofrequency ablation to provide lasting relief.

How We Find the Cause

Advanced Diagnostics for Accurate Treatment

You cannot treat what you cannot see. We utilize a multi-layered diagnostic approach to pinpoint the exact source of your pain.

1. Clinical Evaluation

A detailed review of your medical history, pain characteristics, and a comprehensive physical and neurological examination.

2. Imaging & Tests

We utilize in-house ultrasound, MRI review, and EMG/NCS (nerve conduction studies) to evaluate structural and nerve function.

3. Diagnostic Injections

Sometimes, we use a targeted nerve block as a diagnostic tool. If the injection provides temporary relief, we have confirmed the pain source.

Red Flags

When you should not wait.

While many aches and pains resolve on their own, you should consult a specialist if you experience:

  • Pain that lasts longer than 3 months (chronic pain).
  • Pain that radiates down your arms or legs, accompanied by numbness or weakness.
  • Loss of bowel or bladder control (seek emergency care immediately).
  • Pain that interferes with your sleep, work, or daily activities.
